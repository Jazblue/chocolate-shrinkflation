UK Chocolate Bar Shrinkflation

Weight changes, price rises, and what you’re actually getting — from 2000 to 2026.

Headline: Across 12 major UK chocolate bars, the average bar is roughly 15–20% smaller than it was in the 1990s/2000s. Mars has shrunk by as much as 36% since the 1990s, while headline prices have roughly doubled for many favourites.

Why chocolate is shrinking

  • Cocoa price spike: up ~300% in 2024, hitting $12,000/ton in December 2024; still around $6,000/ton today.
  • Supply shortfall: global cocoa production fell ~14% in 2023–2024 due to bad weather and disease in West Africa.
  • UK impact: chocolate prices rose ~9.7% in one year — more than double overall food inflation.
  • Manufacturer choice: raise prices, cut quality, or shrink sizes. They mostly chose size cuts.

Price impact

  • A bar that was commonly 50p–60p ten years ago is now often 90p–£1.20.
  • That’s roughly a doubling of checkout price for many favourites — before factoring in shrinkage.
  • Galaxy Easter Egg at Asda: £4.98 for 252g → £5.97 for 210g = 44% price rise per gram.
  • Mars at 75p: 51g → 40g while price stayed at 75p = ~27% less chocolate for the same money.

What to check when buying

  • Check price per 100g, not just the shelf price.
  • Compare multipacks carefully — some now contain fewer or smaller bars.
  • Loyalty deals matter — Tesco Clubcard, Sainsbury’s Nectar, and Co-op offers can cut 20–30%.
  • Discounters often win — Aldi, Lidl, B&M, Home Bargains, and Poundland frequently beat the big four on price per gram.
